fastVEP is an open-source Rust reimplementation of Ensembl VEP. This talk has two halves, why you would use it and how to use it, first for annotation and then for ACMG/AMP classification.

The pitch rests on the whole-genome measurement of 7 November 2026 (a call set of GIAB HG002, chromosomes 1 to 22, whose X, Y and mitochondrial calls are not in it): 4,048,342 variants, one thread, an Apple M4, 42.2 s for consequences only and 54.6 s with ClinVar and gnomAD, as medians of five runs (benchmarks/results/v0.5.0/with_vep/summary.tsv). The release shown is the pre-release v0.5.0-rc.1; the latest stable release is v0.4.0.

The problem

Two problems we set out to fix

Annotation is a step a pipeline waits for

109 min
Ensembl VEP 116.3 on one thread, consequences only, for a whole-genome call set (4,048,342 variants of GIAB HG002, chromosomes 1 to 22, GRCh38). Measured on 7 November 2026: slide 4.

A step that takes hours decides how often you re-run it, and what you leave out.

ACMG/AMP criteria are underspecified and fragmented

Underspecified

No guideline says what frequency counts as extremely rare, or how many carriers make a variant observed in a healthy adult. A table and a point scale can give different calls.

Fragmented

The rules are spread over the 2015 standards, a long series of ClinGen recommendations that each change what looked settled, and gene-specific expert-panel specifications.

Sources: benchmarks/results/v0.5.0/with_vep/vep_baseline.csv · manuscript/acmg/manuscript.md (abstract and Section 1, commit 7814589).

Part one is speed: in the head-to-head, Ensembl VEP 116.3 took 6,516 s (109 minutes) on one thread for the whole HG002 call set (chromosomes 1 to 22), summed over one run per chromosome (benchmarks/results/v0.5.0/with_vep/vep_baseline.csv, row wgs_conseq).

Part two is the framing of the ACMG manuscript: the criteria are underspecified (the examples are those of its Introduction) and fragmented (the standards of 2015, ClinGen SVI recommendations, VCEP specifications). Both are taken from manuscript/acmg/manuscript.md, abstract and Section 1, at commit 7814589.

What fastVEP is

Ensembl VEP, reimplemented in Rust

You give

a VCF, gene models (GFF3), the reference (FASTA) and, if you like, annotation databases

fastvep

consequences, HGVS names, supplementary annotations and ACMG/AMP classes

You get

a VCF with a 49-field CSQ, a VEP-style tab file, or JSON

Built to match VEP's output. The consequence and HGVS engine is a port of Ensembl VEP's. The VEP flags it has also work in VEP's underscore spelling, such as --pick_allele. It is not a drop-in for the vep command line (slide 6).
  • Open source (Apache-2.0), written in Rust
  • 49 Sequence Ontology consequence terms are defined and 38 are emitted, structural variants included
  • HGVS g., c. and p. names; gzip inputs; any organism that has a GFF3
  • Two binaries: fastvep to annotate, build databases and filter, and fastvep-web, the web server
Sources: README.md (Features) · docs/VEP_MIGRATION.md · fastvep --help and the contents of the v0.5.0-rc.1 release archive.

fastVEP aims to be compatible with Ensembl VEP and Illumina Nirvana (README, first paragraphs). The sentences about VEP's flags and about the drop-in are from docs/VEP_MIGRATION.md: every flag it shares with VEP is also accepted in VEP's underscore spelling, any other VEP flag is refused, and what it writes is VEP's output; the release binary refuses, for example, --tab, --cache and --fork with error: unexpected argument.

The 49 terms are defined in the code and the predictors emit 38 of them (README, Features). The subcommands of fastvep are those that fastvep --help prints on the v0.5.0-rc.1 release archive: annotate, web, cache, sa-build, sa-convert, filter. The same archive holds a second binary, fastvep-web.

Underspecified

No guideline says what frequency is extremely rare, how many carriers make a variant observed in a healthy adult, or how far into an intron a variant must lie.

Fragmented

The rules are spread over the standards of 2015, a long series of ClinGen recommendations that each change what looked settled, and gene-specific specifications.

Split the combining scheme: the 2015 table has no row for a lone Very Strong criterion, which the points call Likely pathogenic, and points on the benign side would call a lone Supporting criterion Likely benign, which the table does not. Points on both sides, with Likely benign from -1 points (a replay of the recorded criteria, not a run), raise recall of ClinVar's benign-side variants from 66.4% to 77.0% and reference-discordant benign calls from 27 to 399, a trade of 372 more for 10.6 points of recall; the 2015 table on both sides (a real run) gives up 4,900 calls in direction and 5.4 points of pathogenic-side recall to avoid 10 reference-discordant pathogenic calls (Section 3.3).

Apply the conditions that a guideline's text states: the manuscript's ledger codes 64 recorded changes, by one coder from the written record and not independently (21 were a condition of a guideline that the criterion had not applied, among them BS2's full penetrance at an early age, PM1's absence of benign variation, BP1's truncating-only mechanism and PVS1's need for loss of function as the mechanism), and review of the opposite-direction calls was the route by which 30 of the 64 were first found (Sections 2.3, 3.2 and 4.4; change_ledger.csv).

Measure the thresholds that no guideline states: the cutoff for Likely pathogenic, BP7's conservation test and the BS1 and BS2 thresholds matter, each at its own exchange rate between correct calls gained and errors added (for the cutoff, 5 points instead of 6 gives 10,676 more ClinVar pathogenic-side variants called pathogenic-side, at the price of 6,003 more ClinVar VUS and 17 more benign-side variants called pathogenic-side; every step of BP7's conservation threshold buys at least 1,600 correct calls per added error), while the PM2 thresholds under the default scheme and the allele-number floor each move agreement in direction by fewer than a thousand calls of 673,660 (the PM2 threshold: 534,835 calls in direction at strict absence against 534,335 at the default; the floor: 684 calls), and the cutoff for Pathogenic moves exact agreement and not agreement in direction (Sections 3.3 and 4).

Read contradictions beside agreement: the three rules of slide 22 reach 85.6% with 1,817 contradictions, 36 times as many as v38's 51, so agreement alone does not separate cautious from aggressive calling (Section 3.5).

Check each input against an independent listing: ClinGen's export of 30 April 2026, which the benchmark read, does not contain CFTR, nor 103 other curations that ClinGen dates before that day (104 curations in 100 genes); all 558 null variants of CFTR in the benchmark were called VUS and none carried PVS1, and nothing in an aggregate showed it (Sections 3.5 and 4.1; Supplementary Table S7 lists them). The manuscript also recommends a leave-one-out figure beside every ClinVar-derived input: counting a variant's own ClinVar record would add 14.3 points of pathogenic-side recall (Section 3.4).

Count one observation once: in a gene read as dominant, BS2 counts carriers, the count that BS1 and BA1 turn into a frequency, so where both fire they summarize one gnomAD record twice (our reading, not a statement of the ClinGen SVI). Counting both would give 3,432 more exact calls and 638 more calls in direction than v38 and would call TTR p.Val142Ile and VWF p.Arg854Gln, both ClinVar Pathogenic, Benign (Section 3.8).

The last line is the manuscript's own (Section 4, first paragraph): all of this comes from one tool, one reviewer and one benchmark; the bounds are those of slide 28.

ACMG/AMP classification · the bounds

What it is not

fastVEP's classification is a decision-support output for research use. It is not a laboratory-validated classification and does not replace expert review.

1. Closely is relative

“Closely” is relative to the settings that we measured, and a default is not always the setting that agrees most.

2. Tuned on the April set

The thresholds were tuned on the April benchmark, so only the new variants of ClinVar's October release are out of sample. That is a hold-out of variants and almost not of genes.

3. A reference, and one reviewer

ClinVar can be wrong or out of date, and the expert is one reviewer, who saw both calls.

4. 15 of 28 criteria

An input of variants alone, with no genotype, pedigree or phenotype of any individual, allows 15 of the 28 criteria, which caps what any annotator of such an input can reach.

Sources: manuscript/acmg/manuscript.md (commit 7814589), Section 1 (the four bounds), Section 4 and Section 4.4.

The four bounds are those of the manuscript's Introduction (the claim has four bounds) and Discussion, in its words; the first sentence is the last of Section 4.4 (Limitations): fastVEP's classification is a decision-support output for research use, not a laboratory-validated classification, and it does not replace expert review. All of the numbers on the ACMG slides come from one tool, one reviewer and one benchmark (Section 4).

Two further cautions from the same sections: 64% exact and 79% in-direction agreement is not a quality score (Section 4.2), and a VUS from an annotator that has these inputs is a statement about the inputs and does not mean what a VUS from a laboratory means. The manuscript is unpublished (commit 7814589 of the branch docs/acmg-scheme-and-versions); its declarations are not yet completed.
